## Fahd Mirza — "Gemini 4 Argon Tested: I Fell Off My Chair"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5ze8TEWKqOw

Mirza puts Argon through deep, independent hands-on tests — including a Bihari Kebab simulation — and concludes it excels at deep work: sustained, complex reasoning rather than quick chatty answers. Why it matters: quirky real-world probes reveal more than benchmark charts, and this is the other half of yesterday evening's Antigravity demo — the model itself, verified in independent hands.

## Nate B. Jones — "What do AI math and anesthesia have in common...?"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7RjEosTjhE0

Jones reflects on Terence Tao's lecture about OpenAI's AI-generated solutions to hundreds of open math problems — results that work, but that nobody fully understands. His comparison to anesthesia, a medical black box used daily despite incomplete understanding, asks whether mathematics can live with correct-but-inscrutable proofs. Why it matters: this is the verifier question at its deepest — every field is about to get AI results nobody can fully explain, and Tao is asking what "understanding" even means now.

## OpenAI — "How Oracle Uses Codex to Help Business Users Get Answers"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTcK95TAZ-k

Richard Lam, Group Vice President at Oracle Applications Lab, shares how business users — not engineers — describe the outcome they want, and Codex gathers information from internal systems to deliver an analysis, a report, or an application. Why it matters: enterprise AI is shifting from pilot projects to how work gets done, and the users being empowered are non-engineers meeting agents where they are, inside their own company data.
