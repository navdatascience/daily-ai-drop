# The Daily AI Drop — Evening Edition, November 13, 2026

**Listen:** https://muse.ai/podcasts/media/1180242951829162/4c015424-5891-46ff-a366-0f9e546ae170/ep-0894767e-a183-40fe-a4a7-9fcb442e151a.mp3

Six new videos this afternoon, all circling the same question from different directions: who pays, who controls, and who gets to build? A resignation scenario at NVIDIA could move chip prices, the labs are fighting open weights, LangChain raised $300M to govern agents, Mozilla is using AI to defend Firefox, and the open-source side of AI had a very loud afternoon.

## Why Jensen Huang's Resignation Could Change Your AI Bill — Nate Jones

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=04ClMCe_-JY

Nate starts from the premise of Jensen Huang resigning from NVIDIA and traces the chain reaction: what happens to AI chip competition, infrastructure financing, and export rules when the company at the center of it all changes hands at the top. The part that matters for listeners is how the price of compute becomes the price of everything built on top of it — a leadership change at a chip company can show up in your subscription prices months later.

## Open AI just lost — Mehul Mohan

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l5CfpjcfNoA

Mehul picks up the same thread from the open-weights side: the big labs are lined up against open weights, charging for guardrails and controlling the distillation pipeline that smaller models learn from. He walks through paying for guardrails, distillation and GPU access, and a $1.5B settlement, then makes the case for why open weights matter as a counterweight — if the weights are open, nobody can quietly move the goalposts on price or permission.

## LangSmith: The platform to run, improve, and govern agents — LangChain

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NeV6MSN6zEE

LangChain has raised $300 million in a Series C at a $3 billion valuation and is launching the new LangSmith, one platform to run, improve, and govern agents. LangSmith already processes billions of traces a month for more than 7,000 customers. The pitch is that the agents that change how a business runs are the ones whose teams keep iterating on them — and LangSmith gives those teams one place to watch what agents do, test improvements, and put guardrails around them before they touch real customers.

## How Mozilla uses GPT-6 Cyber to find and fix Firefox vulnerabilities — OpenAI

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PkAmyub12K8

Mozilla is combining decades of security engineering with AI agents to strengthen Firefox's defenses. Brian Grinstead joins OpenAI's Clint Gibler to discuss how the team prioritizes investigations, tests and verifies vulnerabilities, finds related bug patterns, and helps engineers develop fixes. Notably, GPT-6 Cyber revealed that some known bugs could be more serious than initially thought — a reminder that the same agent capabilities everyone is racing to govern are also the tools keeping the open web safe.

## ZONOS3-Mini Locally: 100M-Active TTS Model Speaks 43 Languages — Fahd Mirza

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rEMDo90-myc

A hands-on local install and test of ZONOS3-Mini, Zyphra's compact open-source text-to-speech model with 100 million active parameters that speaks 43 languages. For anyone building voice features into an app, or just curious about running models locally, this is the open-weights story in miniature: a small model on your own machine, no API bill, no permission slip.

## Open Source Grok Bot, copy any YouTube video, find anyone online. — The Next New Thing AI

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pnZh5PAF3VE

Andrew Warner and Adam run through the top 10 trending GitHub repositories this week, plus three bonus projects and four community submissions. The headline projects include an open-source Grok bot, a tool to copy any YouTube video, and a way to find anyone online. The through line is taking tools that used to belong to big platforms and rebuilding them in the open.
