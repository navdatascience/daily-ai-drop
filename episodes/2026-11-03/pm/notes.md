# The Daily AI Drop — Evening Edition, November 3, 2026

Microsoft on shipping real AI agents and offline edge AI, OpenAI on privacy, voice banking, code review and hardware-making, plus Google's forward deployed engineering role.

## What does it really take to ship an AI agent? (MicrosoftDeveloper)

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Er0AYcDuZeA

Microsoft walks through the full development cycle of Sparkles, a cupcake-ordering agent built with Microsoft Foundry and the Microsoft Agent Framework — from connecting to a deployed model and loading the shop's persona via MCP prompts, to calling live store tools, inspecting execution traces, and evaluating results. It matters because the unglamorous parts — tracing, evaluation, tool wiring — are where AI demos become real products.

## Glean on privacy and OpenAI Private Intelligence (OpenAI)

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2h16j4K_NbA
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JR7xBi2kJjY

Two short clips with Glean co-founder and CTO T. R. Vishwanath on customer control of data and Glean's work with OpenAI on Private Intelligence, including the Private Safety Processing preview. The through-line: companies will only hand sensitive internal data to more capable AI if they are confident that data stays under their control — and that trust has to be built in practice, not just promised.

## ChatGPT Voice is now in Finances (OpenAI)

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=63pcV4z0DwI

ChatGPT Voice now works with Finances: securely connect your accounts, then talk through what subscriptions you are paying for, which to keep, and what you could save by cancelling. It matters because it turns a chore everyone avoids — digging through statements — into a conversation, the kind of everyday AI assistance that actually sticks.

## Qodo brings company context into Codex code review (OpenAI)

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yC72iQx6rM0

Qodo's Codex plugin brings company context, codebase standards, and independent review into the development workflow, catching bugs across repositories where a change looks right in one codebase and breaks a service in another. Co-founder Itamar Friedman and AI DevRel lead Nnenna Ndukwe discuss building with GPT-6 Astra. Real systems fail at the boundaries between services, so a reviewer that knows the whole map is a genuine step up from single-PR review.

## TamaGPT (OpenAI)

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NCxD-qsQcN0

You do not need to be an engineer to make hardware — GPT-6 Astra can help bring a physical idea to life. The barrier between having an idea for a gadget and actually building one keeps getting lower, which opens hardware creation to people who never thought of themselves as hardware people.

## AI That Works Offline (MicrosoftDeveloper)

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bbz496RR8Xc

An operations technology virtual coworker runs on site, so it keeps working even when the cloud connection drops — Jason Farmer from Microsoft's digital operations team explains, from a conversation on physical AI architecture. In a plant or warehouse, a dropped connection cannot mean a stopped line; this is edge AI where the intelligence lives where the work happens.

## How to Become a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) at Google | Google's Head of FDE Explains (pvergadia)

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yix3vLrtCxk

A live Q&A with Mitesh Agarwal, Managing Director of AI and Forward Deployed Engineering at Google, on how Google builds and scales its FDE teams, the skills the role needs, and how the interview process works. Forward deployed engineers are the people who go inside customer organizations and make AI systems actually work there — one of the fastest-growing roles in AI, and the human bridge behind today's theme of AI doing real work in real places.
