## Matthew Berman — "It's happening..."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JI3bbZNdyeA

Matthew Berman reacts to the biggest AI business news of the year: Anthropic has filed its public S-1 with the SEC, kicking off an IPO that would list its Class A shares on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. The filing reveals $16.3 billion in first-half 2026 revenue — roughly 14x the same period a year earlier — and an $80.7 billion net loss that is mostly a non-cash accounting charge, with an operating loss under $700 million. Price range, share count, and listing date are still blank, with reports pointing to a mid-November debut, and the governance keeps control with founders and a Long-Term Benefit Trust.

## LangChain — "We wired Devin into our Slack to triage messages"

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GNu-d97XY6c

Russell Kaplan of Cognition explains how the company uses Devin internally: triaging Slack messages, knowing who owns what in the codebase, and handling routine engineering work proactively so humans can focus on the bets that change the company's trajectory. The clip comes from Max Agency, LangChain CEO Harrison Chase's show about how the best AI agents are actually being built. It matters because it shows agents crossing from demos into daily workflows — an agent that understands your org chart and backlog well enough to clear the noise before your morning coffee.

## Fahd Mirza — "DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 GSQ+RCO GGUF: Run Under 12GB VRAM Locally"

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ViICJ3_CDZc

Fahd Mirza locally installs and explains a new DeepSeek model: non-uniform GGUF quantizations of a 763-billion-parameter, 384-expert mixture-of-experts model, produced with GSQ and RCO techniques, that runs under 12GB of VRAM. A model of this scale running on a single consumer graphics card would have been a fantasy a year ago, and it keeps your data on your own hardware. The trade-off, as always with quantization, is some loss of precision — but for developers, researchers, and privacy-conscious tinkerers, this is a practical path to serious local AI.

## Microsoft Developer — "Scale One AI Agent Across Sites (Part 2)"

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TRHV_Ze4x5M

Manufacturers don't have to choose between running AI agents in the cloud or on site. Christoph Berlin, Microsoft's Vice President of Engineering for Cloud and AI, continues the series on a hybrid model: one agent framework, built on common data contracts and guardrails, so the same agent runs in the cloud, at the edge, or fully disconnected, with every instance managed through a single interface. It matters because factories and remote sites don't always have reliable connectivity and do have safety and compliance rules a cloud-only agent can't satisfy — if agents are going to run the physical world, this hybrid pattern is how it happens.

## Priyanka Vergadia — "How to Build AI Agents in Claude (No Coding): Full Tutorial"

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8pb6Xe6EZO8

In this step-by-step tutorial, Priyanka Vergadia shows how to create two practical AI agents that automate real business tasks using Claude Desktop, reusable workflows, and simple natural-language instructions — no coding required. Most people use Claude just to ask questions and generate answers; this video is about giving Claude a goal, letting it follow a structured workflow, analyze information, create documents, and deliver finished work. The bigger point is access: the agent era won't belong only to engineers, and if you can describe a process clearly, you can now automate it.
