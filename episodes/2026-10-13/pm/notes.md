## OpenAI's AI Wrote 700 Math Papers. Engineers Still Won't Trust AI With Their Code. — Nate B. Jones

Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pTh4IJ-Mi3Y

OpenAI pointed an unreleased model at roughly 4,000 open math problems and posted 719 AI-generated manuscripts (372 families of results) to GitHub in a single October 6 drop. Mathematicians reacted with open hostility — Oxford's Ursula Martin called it a messy first draft tossed at the community; Columbia's Michael Harris refused to read them — and OpenAI withdrew three papers within a day after a sign error invalidated an argument, revising fourteen more. It matters because this is narrow superintelligence made concrete, yet engineers still keep coding agents out of production: math has a near-free verifier (a proof checks or it doesn't) while production code has to survive real users. The question for your own work: what is your verifier?

## OpenAI Employee Arrested Over Recording Device — Matthew Berman

Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CIlqLzoJiU8

The FBI arrested OpenAI product engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan, 35, a Saudi national at the company since July 2025, on October 9 — charged with using a device to intercept oral communications. On September 30, a routine sweep found an encrypted audio recording device hidden in an executive conference room: inserted into the USB port on the back of a TV, concealed in toilet paper, believed planted around September 28. OpenAI says it used its own AI models to identify who planted it within days, then referred the case to the FBI; more devices were reportedly found in his backpack, and the company believes he meant to plant them across its offices. It matters because frontier AI capability is now a strategic asset — and strategic assets attract insider threats — and because OpenAI reports no indication of model-weight, source-code, or customer-data exfiltration. The lab racing toward superintelligence is learning in public what it costs to protect it.
