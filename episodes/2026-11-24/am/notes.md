# The Daily AI Drop — Morning Edition, November 24, 2026

Alex and Jordan cover a busy overnight in AI: a new open model built for generalized coding, the question of whether AI has just solved mathematics, a complete guide to agent architecture, a billion-dollar robotics founder's vision for physical AI, and a ChatGPT trick that could save you money on holiday gifts.

## GLM-5.4: Specs, Architecture and Wild Demos — Fahd Mirza

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ONhcW73MjGU

Fahd Mirza walks through GLM-5.4, a new model built around generalized coding, covering its specs and architecture before putting it through a series of wild demos. The hands-on tests matter because they show whether the model is genuinely capable or just good at benchmarks. For anyone tracking the open model race, this is the fastest way to see where GLM-5.4 fits.

## Did AI JUST Solve Mathematics? — Matthew Berman

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MkeKXJzJo98

Matthew Berman asks whether AI has crossed the line from being good at math homework to genuinely doing mathematics, walking through a striking new result with help from a 3Blue1Brown explainer. The distinction matters: novel mathematical reasoning would change what research and tutoring look like. A fun, accessible way to get up to speed on where things stand.

## The Complete Guide to AI Agent Architecture: Loops, Harnesses, Tools & MCP — Aishwarya Srinivasan

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o7BBNtnv1Pg

Aishwarya Srinivasan breaks down AI agent architecture from first principles: the difference between a language model, an agent, and its harness, then how agent loops, tools, memory, context engineering, and multi-agent systems come together. The focus is reliability — getting an agent to do the right thing every time, not just once. Essential viewing for anyone building with agents or trying to understand why some AI products feel solid and others feel flaky.

## $1.1B robotics founder: millions of robots will exist in 10 years — Greg Isenberg

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RV9JbYtfCOo

Greg Isenberg sits down with Adrien Gaidon, co-founder and chief strategy officer of Walden Robotics, to map out where the openings are in robotics and physical AI. Gaidon argues robots are now taught with data, and deployment data from robots working in the real world is the most valuable kind — and explains why Walden chose wheels and two-finger grippers for factory work. A grounded reality check on the prediction that millions of robots will exist within ten years.

## Waiting for a better price on that gift? Use ChatGPT. — OpenAI

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=asUuxCqj0GE

OpenAI shows how to use ChatGPT as a price watcher: ask it to check an item's price every day and let you know when it drops, reporting the old price, the new price, and a link to the offer. A small but genuinely useful trick with the holidays coming up — and a good example of AI assistants quietly becoming useful for everyday life admin, not just work.
