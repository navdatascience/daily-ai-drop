## Fahd Mirza — NVIDIA'S Small Model: Nemotron-3.5 Bolt E4B 4GB Locally

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=redCXhIyCJY

Fahd installs and tests NVIDIA's Nemotron-3.5 Bolt, a small language model of only about four gigabytes, running locally on his own machine. A model that small will not beat the frontier giants at everything, but it does not need to — for everyday writing, summarizing, and coding help, local means faster, more private, and free of per-prompt API costs. If you have been curious about running AI at home, this is an approachable place to start.

## Nate Jones — Black October Isn't Just A PHP Bug. Attackers Want Your AI Keys.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qDYe2eTl_D4

AI agents are now finding and exploiting software weaknesses at scale, and a stolen AI API key can run up your bill, because whoever holds the key can spend your compute. Nate walks through what this week's security failures mean for your accounts and what to update, cut, and check this week. Treat API keys like credit card numbers: rotate them, limit what they can do, and watch for usage you did not authorize.

## Mehul Mohan — AI Hacking Everything: Save Yourself

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tYxysep9y7A

Mehul lays out a practical playbook for what he calls the most dangerous time online: use a password manager, generate random passwords, never reuse them, use passkeys whenever possible, and keep cloud storage encrypted. The point is a habit stack, not a single trick — the manager makes random passwords painless, and passkeys remove the password from the equation entirely where supported. If AI has made old attacks faster and cheaper, these basics are the defense that actually scales.

## Mehul Mohan — Save yourself from getting hacked online

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=reKscyYOTrg

A companion video to the one above, carrying the same urgent message: protect yourself before you become the example. Together, Mehul's two afternoon videos frame personal security as something to fix tonight, not someday — starting with the most reused passwords and the accounts that matter most. Pair this with Nate Jones's warning about AI keys, and the evening's message is clear: lock down the keys and the accounts before chasing the next new model.

## Matthew Berman — Kimi K3.1 Invented the Benchmark Scores

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bBqP-w6DHFw

This is a follow-up with a twist on this morning's Kimi K3.1 verdict: Berman now asks whether some of Kimi's benchmark scores were earned or manufactured. Benchmarks are the scoreboard builders and buyers use to decide what to try, fund, and build on — and Kimi is open weight, so many independent builders are making real decisions on these numbers right now. The takeaway is not to declare the model good or bad in one evening, but to demand the evidence behind the number: what test, under what conditions, and can anyone else reproduce it?
