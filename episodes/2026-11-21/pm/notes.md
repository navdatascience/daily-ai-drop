Five new videos this Saturday afternoon, and they all circle the same question from different angles: what happens when AI tools get this good, this fast, and this close to home? Alex and Jordan cover a frontier model that runs on your laptop, a design-first workflow for coding agents, Anthropic's new life as a public company, Reid Hoffman on preparing for AI at work, and Microsoft's packed Ignite keynote.

## Most People Are Completely UNPREPARED FOR AI! — Aishwarya Srinivasan

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YAGhc-uApas

Aishwarya sits down with Reid Hoffman to talk about what happens to work when AI takes on more coding, writing, and problem solving. They dig into agentic AI, the roles employers will actually hire for going forward, and why being mid-career may feel especially uncertain right now. It matters because it puts words to the anxiety many professionals feel — and Hoffman's message is about preparation, not fear: learn to work with the tools early and stay flexible about what your job actually is.

## Google's Elephant-Goldfish Model: Design First, Code Last — Fahd Mirza

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gl8ecFo9X80

A hands-on tutorial on the Elephant-Goldfish model, an open-source plugin for designing and reviewing software with agents. The idea is simple: do the thinking and design work before ever letting an agent write code, so the agent keeps context instead of forgetting it mid-build. If you have ever been burned by an agent that built the wrong thing very quickly, this is the workflow fix worth trying.

## Frontier Open AI on YOUR Laptop (Inkling 2 Mini) — Mehul Mohan

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=58uI2Zwdv14

Mehul breaks down Inkling 2 Mini, a model built on a looped transformer architecture that reuses layers to stay small while punching above its weight. He covers benchmarks against DeepSeek, a one-million-token context window running on a laptop, a head-to-head game test, and why open weights matter. It matters because the gap between what big labs offer and what you can run yourself keeps shrinking — changing who gets to build with serious models.

## I'm Crashing Out About Anthropic — Matthew Berman

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I0Fasi1-teQ

Anthropic is now a public company, and Matthew examines how its warnings about AI risk square with the pitch it has to make to Wall Street. He also looks at why Anthropic restricted Claude subscription access through tools like OpenClaw and what METR found about AI accelerating Anthropic's own research. It matters because how Anthropic handles the tension between safety-first branding and shareholder expectations will likely set the template for every AI lab that goes public after it.

## POV: Microsoft woke up and dropped crazy AI features — Aishwarya Srinivasan

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SkymljXhqDk

Aishwarya recaps Satya Nadella's Microsoft Ignite keynote, where Microsoft packed what felt like a year's worth of AI announcements into a single event. The headline feature is Copilot Autopilot, an agent that handles recurring work, follows up on emails, and keeps projects moving, rolling out to Frontier customers. It matters because Microsoft is shipping AI features across its whole stack at a pace that is hard to keep up with — and the agent era is arriving inside the tools people already use every day.
