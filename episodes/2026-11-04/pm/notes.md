## GPT-Image-2.6: Faster image generation with Flare and Sunburst — OpenAI

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9ZxCOHkfn_8

OpenAI is bringing GPT-Image-2.6 to Codex and the API, with the 2.6 versions of Flare and Sunburst generating images 50% faster than before. Flare is for quick first passes — at low image quality it can produce an image in as little as six seconds — while Sunburst at medium or high quality keeps details consistent across edits. It matters because image generation is becoming infrastructure: speed decides whether developers build on it, and edit consistency decides whether the results are usable for real work.

## Messi asks ChatGPT to reimagine his room — OpenAI

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6IttBmI4P9M

Messi had an empty room he wanted to feel more like him, but wasn't sure where to start, so he asked ChatGPT for ideas with the prompt "Reimagine this room in my style. Surprise me." It matters because it shows AI design help going mainstream: you describe the feeling you want rather than the exact specifications, and the model proposes something. For most people, this conversational route is how AI assistance will actually enter daily life.

## How Figma Is Building Self-Serve Analytics with OpenAI — OpenAI

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0LDs5rvx1EI
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=44GBqQGc-v0

In an early pilot with OpenAI, Figma is building shared context that helps marketers ask how campaigns are landing and whether they are converting customers, instead of waiting for someone to build a dashboard or metric. It matters because the bottleneck in most organizations isn't the model, it's the waiting: the handoffs and dashboards that have to be built before anyone can answer a simple question. Removing that wait is what turns AI capability into actual productivity.

## Browser Use with Managed Deep Agents — LangChain

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r1iJzoxFBtg

A lot of the work you'd want an agent to do happens on sites with no API, like filling out forms and shopping, and Managed Deep Agents now makes it easy to give an agent a browser in two lines of code, with optimized browser-use tools under the hood. It matters because browser automation has historically been fiddly and fragile, and agents that can't click through a form can't finish real jobs no matter how good their plans are. This is the unglamorous tooling that turns agent demos into products.

## If You Want To Create Wealth In The Age of AI, Watch This — Greg Isenberg

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b3tMAOFB71o

In this solo episode, Greg Isenberg lays out how he thinks about building an AI-proof business now that anyone can spin up a company in seconds, products get copied pixel for pixel, and layoffs keep coming. His framework is eight hard-to-copy categories: atoms, permission, attention, timing, belonging, liquidity, unique data, and outcomes. It matters because when software itself becomes cheap and instant, durable advantage moves to everything around the software — community, distribution, data, and real-world operations — and that's a healthier way to think about a moat than hoping your model stays ahead.
