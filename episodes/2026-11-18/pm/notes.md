## The Robot Brain Might Already Exist. It's A Coding Agent. — Nate B. Jones

**Link:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkvoUCgHP6Q

Coding agents are moving from software to physical hardware, enabling robots to perform tasks without specific training. A Stanford study shows a robot navigating environments using only code-based instructions rather than traditional task-specific training. It matters because every improvement in coding models could now become an improvement in robotics automatically — the robot brain may not be a separate research program anymore.

## Fireside Chat With Alex Atallah, OpenRouter — LangChain

**Link:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6UJPFP09lro

Alex Atallah, CEO and co-founder of OpenRouter, explains how teams choose among models and providers: why companies tend to add model families over time, how he sorts work into low, medium, and high entropy tasks to decide when you need a frontier model, and why a different model family should check an agent's output. Worth watching if you are picking models for a real product — model choice is becoming a routing problem, not a loyalty problem.

## Offline and Online Evals Work Like Hiring a Teammate — LangChain

**Link:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FWk1txbbdiY

Jane Xue, Executive Director at JPMorgan Chase, explains how her team builds and maintains trust in agents that can be deployed overnight, starting with offline evals that ask what an agent should and shouldn't do. She compares the process to hiring and developing a teammate, where the first offline eval is the interview and online evals give continuous feedback. It reframes evals from a technical chore into a management discipline — essential viewing if your agents can ship to production overnight.

## LangSmith for Coding Agents: Track Spend by Outcome — LangChain

**Link:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dg2Z6IRbDVI

Coding is the largest category of enterprise AI application spend, reaching $34B in 2026, but most engineering leaders still can't give an exact answer to what it's buying. LangSmith for Coding Agents ties coding agent spend to the pull requests it produces, with a new concept called outcomes linking GitHub pull requests or GitLab merge requests to threads. This is the grown-up version of the agent story: not whether agents can code, but what each merged pull request cost.

## Introducing ChatGPT for Accounting — OpenAI

**Link:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AhmCKHNqhB8

ChatGPT for Accounting helps take the busywork out of the month-end close. It finds completed jobs in email and drafts QuickBooks invoices for review, flagging anything unclear, and also reconciles transactions, processes invoices and receipts, and prepares monthly reports using existing spreadsheets and connected accounting tools. The unglamorous, repetitive, high-stakes work is exactly the kind of thing a careful agent can take over — every small business gets a bookkeeper that never sleeps.
