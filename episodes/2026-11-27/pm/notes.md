# The Daily AI Drop — Evening Edition, November 27, 2026

Six new videos this afternoon, and they tell a coherent story: open-source runtimes and tools are getting serious, the harness around an agent matters as much as the model inside it, there are hard limits on what brute force can do, and the human questions — what work is worth doing, and how to get good at it — are still the ones that matter most.

## Fahd Mirza — OpenShell Tutorial: Safe, Private Runtime for Autonomous AI Agents

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5oVfDCLRBPw

OpenShell is a runtime for running fleets of autonomous AI agents locally and privately, on your own machines, instead of handing them over to somebody else's cloud. Fahd installs it, tests it, and walks through how it keeps agents sandboxed and contained so they can act without wandering off. If you are building anything with agents that touches real data or real systems, the trust problem this solves is the one standing between a demo and something you would actually deploy.

## Aishwarya Srinivasan — AI Agent vs Agent Harness

https://www.youtube.com/shorts/c9jN4xxtscg

A quick short that clears up a distinction a lot of people get tangled in: the agent is the thing doing the work — reasoning, calling tools, making decisions — while the harness is the scaffolding around it, the loops, the guardrails, the memory, and the way it recovers when something goes wrong. It explains why two agents built on the same model can behave completely differently, and it gives you the vocabulary to follow the conversation around this morning's story about Google's agent rewriting its own harness.

## Mehul Mohan — AI Is About To Destroy EVERYTHING

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ANhoEQOfZmA

Despite the dramatic title, this is a guided tour of P versus NP, one of the biggest open questions in computer science. Mehul starts with something simple, like sorting a list, builds up to Sudoku — hard to solve but easy to check — and explains why brute force explodes so fast that even the fastest computers cannot save you. It is really about limits: so much AI hype pretends more compute solves everything, and P versus NP is the formal version of why that is not true.

## TheNextNewThingAI — Open Source: Adobe, browser gets past blocks, Apple's design style, and more

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qM7dlXtgZwU

Ten incredible open-source GitHub projects, including an Adobe replacement, a browser tool that gets past blocks, and a project that brings Apple's design style to your own apps. The running theme is that a lot of paid software now has a free, open alternative you can run yourself — and open source is becoming the place where the interesting infrastructure lives, whether that is runtimes, browsers, or design systems.

## Nate B. Jones — Is AI Just Making You Faster At A Job You Never Wanted? 4 Steps To Find A Better Use For It.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sVz7k4rlXU8

Nate asks whether AI is just making you faster at a job you never wanted, then lays out four steps for finding a better use for it — how to find AI work worth trying and, just as importantly, people to learn it with. The default AI productivity advice is all about doing the same thing ten percent faster; Nate asks the bigger question of whether you are pointed in the right direction at all. Worth sitting with over the weekend.

## Aish Reganti — OpenAI Engineer: Fastest way to become an AI Engineer in 2027

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QSWCHXccCC4

Aish talks with Kiriti, an engineer who spent over a decade building AI systems, worked on Google's massive ads infrastructure, was a founding engineer at a startup NVIDIA acquired, and most recently worked on OpenAI's Codex team. They break down how to become an AI engineer in 2027, whether you really need a master's degree or PhD, which skills actually matter, and how to build projects that get you hired. The headline takeaway: the degree matters a lot less than people think, and the projects you build matter a lot more.
