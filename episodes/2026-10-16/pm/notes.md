# The Daily AI Drop — Evening Edition, October 16, 2026

Alex and Jordan break down five afternoon drops: OpenAI's Dot triaging a 3am on-call page from a phone and ChatGPT running shared team tasks in the cloud, LangChain's Charlie O'Neill on why continual learning is still unsolved, Fahd Mirza testing NVIDIA's 74-billion-parameter Nemotron locally, and the week's top open-source AI repos.

## OpenAI — "Put your dot on call"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GBOZIRK1ujw

An OpenAI engineer triaged a 3am on-call incident entirely from his phone: his Dot investigated the issue and started a Codex thread so he could steer the fix from the ChatGPT app. It's the latest proof point in the Dots arc — from killing zombie meetings to calendar triage — showing persistent agents handling real operational work. If an agent can handle incident response at 3am, the bar for everyday delegation keeps rising.

## OpenAI — "Put ChatGPT to work on shared team tasks"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OMxEUdIgsQc

A demo of shared, recurring team tasks in ChatGPT: a legal team's daily intake workflow where the team reviews requests, checks deadlines, and refines shared instructions together while the task runs on a schedule in the cloud. Available on Enterprise plans. The shift that matters here is from personal assistant to shared teammate — multiple people feeding one improving workflow.

## LangChain — "Why Nobody Has Solved Continual Learning"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=whnDiwcL4HE

Charlie O'Neill (Co-Head of Model Training at Baseten) explains why the "update" step in the own-your-intelligence loop remains unsolved: the SFT-vs-RL trade-off, catastrophic forgetting when new training erases old skills, and distillation warm starts. Recorded at LangChain Presents: The Learning Loop in San Francisco. For anyone shipping agents in production, this is why models go stale — everyone has solved training, nobody has solved retraining.

## Fahd Mirza — "NVIDIA Nemotron-3.5 Bolt-74B-A9B Locally: It's Not Over"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B4HQ-c8i3NM

Fahd installs and tests NVIDIA's Nemotron 3.5 Bolt — a 74-billion-parameter vision-language model built for everyday agentic tasks — on local hardware. Paired with this morning's 2.4GB Ternary Bonsai test on the same channel, it's a same-day look at both ends of the local-model spectrum. The local scene keeps narrowing the gap with hosted giants, and Fahd's channel is the running scoreboard.

## The Next New Thing — "GitHub's top: Copy any app, create gorgeous videos, 3d print anything & more"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kqn_mSjSWMs

Andrew Warner and Adam round up the week's top GitHub repos, headlined by REA (Reverse Engineer Anything) — the AI that opens up software on your computer and explains it. This is the third channel in a week to feature REA, which is the signal. Also covered: Hyperframes for AI-generated video from HTML, Ghidra (the NSA's decompiler), and Text to CAD. The recurring theme: the open-source shelf now has a free answer for a shocking number of paid tools.
