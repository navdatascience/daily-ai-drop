## Nate B. Jones — How to choose model vs effort

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZVnuhgXF9uM

A quick short breaking down a choice you make every time you use AI: the model and the effort level feel like one dial, but they're two. The model should match the problem's difficulty — a lighter model is fine for a deck or an email, while a hard problem you want to argue through needs a stronger one. Effort is how hard that model thinks: choosing the model is like picking the car, setting effort is like giving it more gas. Most of us conflate the two, throwing the biggest model at trivial tasks or a weak model at problems it can't solve. Getting the split right saves money, latency, and bad answers.

## Aishwarya Srinivasan — Google's VP of Engineering on Preparing Enterprise Data for AI | Chris Pinkney

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=63thtuCVUw0

A conversation with Chris Pinkney, Google Fellow and co-author of Google Spanner, on the problem every big company is quietly choking on: enterprise data isn't ready for AI. The core argument is that graph databases and knowledge graphs bridge the gap — traditional RAG falls short because answers in a real business live in the relationships between things (which customer owns which account, which system feeds which product), not in isolated documents. Knowledge graphs give AI agents that structured context to reason with instead of a pile of similar-looking text chunks. If you work anywhere near enterprise AI, this is the clearest explanation of why agents keep hitting walls inside big companies — and a direct tie-in to the show's week-long arc on agents being only as good as their context.
