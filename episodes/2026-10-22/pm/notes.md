# The Daily AI Drop — Evening Edition, October 22, 2026

Ten new videos dropped this afternoon, and a theme emerged: memory. Who remembers what, and whether we can trust what is inside the black box. Alex and Jordan cover Uber's company-wide ChatGPT Work rollout, a local memory-writing model for agents, the Goodfire interpretability essay that has researchers arguing, plus a twenty-minute AI system design crash course, monetizing your agents, and Microsoft's five-video masterclass on the data layer underneath production agents.

### OpenAI — "Uber rolls out ChatGPT Work to 30,000+ employees"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l16Gw7oVLNQ

Uber is expanding its OpenAI partnership from a one-thousand-employee pilot to its entire global workforce of more than thirty thousand people. Roughly eighty percent of the pilot users were non-engineers in finance, operations, and product, using the agent for operations data analysis, localizing training materials, and building personal dashboards. It matters as the clearest enterprise agent adoption signal yet: tools in everyone's hands, room to experiment, and leadership watching what gets built. The expanded partnership also covers an AI assistant for drivers and voice-based ride booking.

### Fahd Mirza — "Hy-Memory-30B-A3B + Hy Memory: Local Memory Writer for AI Agents"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QznWfqdbOj0

A hands-on test of a thirty-billion-parameter model that runs locally and whose whole job is writing and organizing memory for AI agents. It matters because memory is becoming the layer everyone is fighting over — following Claude Mem's shared memory and Claude Duet's personal agent — and this one keeps your agent's memories on your own machine with no cloud service or subscription. The unglamorous infrastructure that quietly makes persistent agents work.

### Nate B. Jones — "I Read Goodfire's New Essay. AI May Be Far Easier To Understand Than We Thought."
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=15gxN8DnC8s

A guided tour of Goodfire researcher Nick Cammarata's October fifteenth essay "Neural Networks Are Already Clean," a twenty-six-thousand-word argument that model internals are far more readable than the field believed and that superposition was largely mistaken. The famous cat-and-car neuron gets re-read as a V-shape neuron, and a preliminary audit estimates about ninety percent of neuron poles in Qwen3-32B have one main job. It matters because readable neurons would make interpretability practical — the tool safety researchers want for spotting deception — though researchers like Neel Nanda and Leo Gao dispute key claims and the technical paper is still weeks away.
