## GPT-6.2 Astra got brutal reviews. Gemini 4 Neon got raves. Here's why both are right. — Nate B. Jones

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y0WVOisJ6Js

Nate breaks down why the two new models are splitting user opinion. Astra draws complaints from many users while Neon earns praise for being fun and optimistic to work with — and Nate argues both reactions are valid. The lesson is that model quality depends heavily on the job you give it, so the reviews tell you more about fit than about a single ranking.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AewmWwkLTvw

Fahd installs and tests Surogate 3.7, a model built for enterprise agentic applications. It is not trying to beat frontier models at coding; its strength is reliable multi-step workflow automation. Worth watching if you are choosing a model for real business processes rather than benchmark scores.

## NEW GLM 5.4 is... very unique! — Mehul Mohan

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FVTqz1lCzWQ

Mehul tests GLM-5.4, a 750-billion-parameter model built on a loop transformer architecture with a KV cache design that keeps it fast. He runs demos, checks access and pricing, and puts it through a Mario game test — including eleven minutes of thinking on the hardest setting — then compares it directly against Gemini 4 Neon on the same task. A hands-on look at one of the most architecturally unusual models of the year.

## Jev Explained: How TypeSafe's System One Model Fits into Your Agents — LangChain

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0vz5slHWAp8

Sydney Runkle talks with Sasha Sheng, co-founder of TypeSafe AI, about Jev — a model that returns typed answers with a confidence score instead of generated text. It breaks a fuzzy problem into small questions answered in parallel, which makes it a natural fit for agents that query real data and need to be right. A useful follow-up to this morning's short clip comparing a plain LLM with Jev on database queries.

## We Open Sourced Our Design Brain — LangChain

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=emId9_O2sU8

LangChain open sourced Content Studio, the design agent it runs in Slack, which has already produced more than 3,000 assets including work for major launches. Every graphic it makes is an HTML page and every video is an animation timeline, so the output stays editable and on-brand. The video walks through how it is built and how to deploy it to your own Slack workspace in three steps.

## Who's Your Agent Acting For? Managed Deep Agents in Under 65 Seconds — LangChain

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fMA3UavzbLo

Victor Moreira explains how Managed Deep Agents define an agent's access by the channel it is called from — so an agent can use your personal memories and credentials in a private message while keeping that information out of a shared channel. When the agent lacks a user's on-behalf-of token, a built-in human-in-the-loop step kicks in. Essential viewing if you are deploying agents where real people work.

## The Complete LangChain Ecosystem Explained — LangChain

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0wr3zk5gPRM

A full tour of the LangChain ecosystem, from its October 2022 origins as an open-source project for chaining prompts and PDFs to today's complete agent development lifecycle: LangGraph for orchestration, the LangChain framework's customizable tool-calling loop, Deep Agents as a ready-made harness, and LangSmith for observability. The map if you have lost track of how the pieces fit together.

## Investigating Linux Vulnerabilities with GPT-6 Cyber — OpenAI

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MpsO8Ds8uyo

GPT-6 Cyber helped validate 948 Linux kernel vulnerabilities and generate candidate patches for human review. Cyber researcher Kyle Zeng sits down with Clint Gibler to explain the vulnerability research pipeline he built. The point is not that AI replaces security teams — it moves them faster from finding a flaw to getting a fix into the software people rely on.

## How Franklin Templeton uses AI for investment research | OpenAI — OpenAI

## Google Antigravity in action: Building an offline wildlife field guide — Google Developers

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fpQcDQJQLhg

## Robots are the new apps (and you're early) — Greg Isenberg

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TXzJ4pGW59o

Greg sits down with Adrien Gaidon, co-founder and chief strategy officer of Walden Robotics, to map where the openings are in robotics and physical AI. Teams now teach robots with data, and deployment data carries the most value; Walden chose wheels and two-finger grippers for factory work. The conversation digs into the opportunities around the big labs — scarce-access data and supervision — and makes the case that physical AI is the next app-store-sized opening.
