## Fahd Mirza — [Kimi K3.1 Pro: Most Capable Open-Weight Model Now](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XwTNOdTcWHA)

Hands-on test of Moonshot AI's Kimi K3.1 Pro, a three-trillion-parameter-class open-weight model, through image, coding, and reasoning tasks. It matters because the closed-vs-open gap is shrinking from years to weeks — capable open weights make serious AI cheaper, more private, and less locked into one vendor's cloud.

## Matthew Berman — [Team AMA (and Kimi K3.1 Testing!!)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7fZ1rFcm1nA)

Live team AMA with hands-on Kimi K3.1 testing — the second practical channel testing this model on the same day. It matters as an independent check on Fahd's result: when two creators stress-test the same open model on release day, the hype gets a reality check.

## Matthew Berman — [Who is local AI really for?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fHY0sjAejcA)

The honest version of the local-model debate: who actually needs to run models at home, and when does the cloud just win? It matters as a reality check before you buy hardware you don't need — privacy, cost, and control users should go local; everyone else is fine in the cloud.

## Matthew Berman — [Sam Altman on Why Your Most Important Advice Should Come From People](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f8uIINtoJCM)

A clip of Sam Altman arguing your most important advice should come from people, not machines. A human note worth hearing in a week when everything else on the feed is machines giving advice.

## OpenAI — [How Benefit Street Partners is building financial analysis tools with Codex](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTkDi1THc_g) + [From analyst to builder with Codex | Benefit Street Partners](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qyGvwLgqmaU)

Anant Kumar at Benefit Street Partners built a tool that collects loan valuations from SEC filings and turns them into a website with visualizations — the first version took about a day. It matters because of his line: "I've gone from being just an analyst and a portfolio manager to being a developer as well now." The analyst-to-builder arc, in one working example.

## OpenAI — [How AT&T is building AI-powered legal workflows with OpenAI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V39_L7uh66M)

AT&T's legal team describes a natural-language intake system that would classify and prioritize legal matters and connect them with relevant knowledge. It matters as the enterprise pattern repeating across industries: AI handles triage, humans spend time on strategy and judgment.

## OpenAI — [How Shield uses OpenAI's Astra to support IT teams](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m6MlxaFMmPI) + [AI agents for everyday IT fixes | Shield and OpenAI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2lNUGkCg_qc)

Shield is building Forge, where AI agents use OpenAI's Astra to work directly in business software and resolve everyday IT problems (login, connectivity) without a technician. Shield reports 35–40% faster software navigation on average. It matters because routine IT support is exactly the kind of high-volume, interrupt-driven work agents are made for.

## OpenAI — [How Realpage. builds AI training with ChatGPT Work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hgX1hgyWD4g) + [From notes to AI training with ChatGPT Work | Realpage.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Cs5qyBlYQk)

Austin Braham at Realpage. uses ChatGPT Work to turn rough ideas, notes, and source materials into complete AI training packages tailored to different teams, roles, and skill levels. It matters as proof that enablement at scale — consistent education across a whole company — is a real agent use case, not just a demo.

## OpenAI — [Hunt bugs with your dot](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6f7ojkd4_Wc)

Engineer Rennie Song shows how she uses dots (OpenAI's agent tool) to build better dots: gathering feedback from social media and user reports, updating a live tracker in a ChatGPT Space, and investigating and fixing bugs with Codex. It matters because it's honest dogfooding — real bug triage, in the open, with the agent tool doing the work.

## LangChain — [The Boring Version of Continual Learning](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=StDAmjuQOE4)

Will Brown of Prime Intellect makes the case that continual learning is about to get boring — in a good way. It's becoming the software development life cycle applied to models: workflows grow from terminal experiments into production software, evals should capture the vibe check, and he walks through the post-training methods he reaches for (SFT, on-policy methods, context distillation). It matters because boring means repeatable, and repeatable means you can ship it.

## Aish Reganti — [Want an FDE job? Learn these 3 layers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJdEhqQgrek)

Forward-deployed engineering is three layers of skills, stacked: software at the base, AI in the middle, and the people side on top. It matters as a career roadmap timed perfectly to this afternoon's enterprise stories — the jobs exist because companies are deploying; the people layer (working with customers, translating business to model) is what gets you deployed, not just hired.
