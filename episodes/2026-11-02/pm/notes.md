## It's Official: Qwen Will Always Be Open-Sourced — fahdmirza

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ia8rFZqi004

Qwen's team has publicly committed to keeping Qwen 4 and Qwen 5 open, so the current generation and the next one both stay available for anyone to download, study, and build on. That matters most for people who can't afford big GPUs or paid AI subscriptions, since open models are what make local, budget-friendly AI possible. Coming the same day as the morning edition's look at running big models on a single GPU at home, this commitment is what keeps that local-AI future alive.

## Coinbase on OpenAI Private Intelligence — OpenAI

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NbDF90z-g6w
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Amm9ySSET4c

Coinbase CTO Rob Witoff explains why security, guardrails, and zero data retention matter when a company handling sensitive financial information uses OpenAI's models — summed up in his line, "It's still our data." OpenAI Private Intelligence is meant to give businesses stronger controls over their data while they use the most capable models, built with some of OpenAI's largest customers. For any company that has held back on AI over data worries, Coinbase is the case study in how the trust problem gets solved — and vendors that solve it will win the enterprise.

## The $1M+ Solo Grok Bot Business (Full Course) — GregIsenberg

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=67BmkptgTms

Greg sits down with Nick, co-founder of Orgo, to break down how to build a custom Grok Bot for a business and sell it for $25,000 up front with a $3,000-a-month retainer. Nick demos Bops, the agent platform he built in a 36-hour weekend, where each bot has its own phone number, email, and computer with four screens. The most stealable lesson is how to pick a niche: call 50 people first, find the painful problem, and only then build the bot for it.

## Big Tech is shifting, and you need to pay attention — NateBJones

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eDafFeXAOYk

On October 27, Salesforce made Microsoft's AI agents easier to use, and the same day Oracle said it's bringing its apps into ChatGPT — rivals opening doors for rivals, and nobody does that out of generosity. Nate's point is that the platform landscape is shifting under anyone choosing an AI vendor right now, so buyers should ask hard questions about interoperability before committing. He lays out 8 questions to ask any AI agent vendor, with the through-line matching tonight's other stories: companies want control over their data and freedom from lock-in.
